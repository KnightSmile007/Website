Frequently Asked Questions.

Straightforward answers to common queries regarding GST registration, return deadlines, ITR filings, and seamless remote onboarding.

GST registration is statutory once your annual turnover exceeds ₹40 Lakhs for goods suppliers (₹20 Lakhs in Special Category States) or ₹20 Lakhs for service providers (₹10 Lakhs in Special Category States). Furthermore, registration is mandatory irrespective of turnover for e-commerce sellers, interstate suppliers, and casual taxable persons.

💡 Turnover limits: ₹40L (Goods) / ₹20L (Services) / Mandatory for E-commerce & Interstate trade.

Yes. Filing a Nil Return for both GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B is legally mandatory even during periods of zero business activity. Failing to file attracts a statutory late fee of ₹20 per day (₹10 CGST + ₹10 SGST) under Section 47, and prolonged non-filing can result in suspension or cancellation of your GSTIN.

⚖️ Section 47 levies ₹20/day on Nil returns. Never leave a tax period unfiled.

The Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme allows registered taxpayers with aggregate annual turnover up to ₹5 Crore to file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B on a quarterly basis instead of monthly. However, tax liability must still be deposited monthly using Challan PMT-06 by the 25th of the month (via either the 35% Fixed Sum method or Self-Assessment method).

⚡ Eligibility: Turnover up to ₹5 Cr. File returns quarterly, deposit tax monthly via PMT-06.

Under Section 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act, a buyer can only claim Input Tax Credit if the supplier has furnished invoice details in their GSTR-1 or IFF and the credit is reflected in the buyer's auto-generated GSTR-2B statement. Provisional ITC or unverified claims in GSTR-3B trigger automated demand notices from the GST system.

⚖️ Section 16(2)(aa): 100% GSTR-2B matching is statutory for claiming valid ITC.

For salaried and individual taxpayers, primary documents include Form 16 from your employer, Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS downloaded from the IT portal, bank account statements for the financial year, capital gains statements from broking accounts, and proof of tax deductions under Chapter VI-A (such as Section 80C, 80D, and NPS).

📁 Quick turnarounds: 24 - 48 hours with Form 16, AIS/26AS and bank statements.

The New Tax Regime under Section 115BAC is the default regime with lower tax slab rates, a ₹75,000 standard deduction, and full tax rebate up to ₹7 Lakhs taxable income, but it restricts Chapter VI-A deductions (80C, 80D, HRA). The Old Tax Regime remains advantageous if your total eligible deductions and housing loan interest exceed approximately ₹3.75 - ₹4 Lakhs. We compute both side-by-side to minimize your tax liability legally.

💡 Default New Regime rebate up to ₹7L. We run a comparative computation for maximum savings.

If you miss the initial deadline, you can file a Belated Return under Section 139(4) up until December 31st of the assessment year. A statutory late fee under Section 234F applies (₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if total income is below ₹5 Lakhs), alongside 1% per month interest under Section 234A on unpaid tax. Furthermore, losses from business or capital assets (other than house property) cannot be carried forward.

⚠️ Section 234F late fee applies up to Dec 31st. Unfiled returns cannot carry forward losses.

TDS deducted during any calendar month must be deposited into the government account via Challan ITNS 281 on or before the 7th of the subsequent month. Delaying payment attracts mandatory penal interest under Section 201(1A) at 1.5% per month (or part of a month) from the actual date of deduction until the deposit date.

⚠️ Section 201(1A): 1.5% interest per month on delayed TDS challan deposits.

While Form 16 is an annual salary TDS certificate from an employer, Form 16A is a quarterly TDS certificate issued for non-salary payments, including professional fees (Section 194J), contract payments (Section 194C), rent (Section 194I), and bank fixed deposit interest (Section 194A). Deductors must issue Form 16A within 15 days of filing their quarterly TDS returns.

📄 Form 16A covers non-salary TDS deductions and must reconcile with Form 26AS.

Under Section 197 of the Income Tax Act, taxpayers who anticipate a low or nil total tax liability (due to carry-forward losses or high allowable business expenses) can apply online via Form 13 on TRACES for a Lower or Nil TDS deduction certificate. Once granted by the Assessing Officer, deductors must deduct TDS at the reduced rate specified in the certificate.

🏛️ Section 197 application via Form 13 on TRACES prevents excessive working capital blockage.

Under Section 44AB of the Income Tax Act, tax audit is mandatory for businesses having gross turnover exceeding ₹1 Crore (or ₹10 Crore if digital/cash transactions are under 5%). For professionals (freelancers, doctors, architects, consultants), tax audit applies if gross receipts exceed ₹50 Lakhs (or ₹75 Lakhs under presumptive Section 44ADA subject to 95% digital receipts).

📊 Business: ₹1 Cr / ₹10 Cr (95% digital). Professionals: ₹50L / ₹75L (digital).

Every registered Indian Private Limited Company and One Person Company (OPC) must complete mandatory annual compliances with the Registrar of Companies (ROC), including Form AOC-4 (Financial Statements), Form MGT-7/MGT-7A (Annual Return), and annual DIR-3 KYC verification for all active directors. Failure to file incurs a heavy recurring penalty of ₹100 per day per form with no maximum limit.

📑 AOC-4, MGT-7 and DIR-3 KYC. Avoid ₹100/day ongoing statutory default penalties.
